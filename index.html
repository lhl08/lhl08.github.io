Research

LEXGR: Extending Flexible LLM-Based Keyboard Decoding to New Words
Tony Li, Ziang Chen, Paul Zhu, Yan Ma, Xiaojun Bi
Under review

LEXGR lets users add new names and terms to an LLM-based keyboard without word-specific examples or retraining. Its candidate-conditioned decoder supports the new vocabulary across taps, gestures, multi-stroke gestures, and mixed tap-gesture input.

DecoLego: A Functional Framework for Probing, Recomposing, and Extending Keyboard Decoders
Ziang Chen*, Tony Li*, Paul Zhu, Xiaojun Bi
Under review

DecoLego is a functional framework for understanding and extending keyboard decoders. It separates signal, lexicon, and language evidence into testable components, supporting controlled diagnosis, recomposition, and adaptation to additional input forms.

Decoding without Profiling: Privacy-Preserving Gesture Typing through Backtracking
Federico Newton, Tony Li, Xiaojun Bi, Luis A. Leiva
Under review

This work introduces backtracking, a lightweight, training-free transformation that obscures motion patterns linked to user profiling while preserving the spatial path needed for gesture decoding. It reduces attribute inference to chance levels while retaining up to 98% of Top-1 recognized words.

Structure Before Prose: A Persistent Argument Representation for Source-Grounded Legal Writing
Ziang Chen, Paul Zhu, Yazheng Chen, Tony Li, Xianfeng David Gu, Xin Miao, Lijun Yu, Xiaojun Bi
Under review

Arbor supports source-grounded legal writing through a persistent, expert-editable argument structure that coordinates generation, provenance, verification, and revision. The same hierarchy lets attorneys organize evidence, regenerate bounded sections, and trace cited claims back to source documents.

KeySense: LLM-Powered Hands-Down, Ten-Finger Typing on Commodity Touchscreens
Tony Li, Yan Ma, Zhuojun Li, Chun Yu, IV Ramakrishnan, Xiaojun Bi
ACM CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI), 2026

KeySense turns commodity touchscreens into hands-down ten-finger keyboards by distinguishing resting contacts from intentional keystrokes and decoding them with a fine-tuned large language model. It reuses physical-keyboard muscle memory, enabling fast, low-fatigue text entry without extra hardware.

BIT: Battery-free, IC-less and Wireless Smart Textile Interface and Sensing System
Weiye Xu, Tony Li, Yuntao Wang, Xing-Dong Yang, Te-Yen Wu
ACM CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI), 2025

BIT is a smart textile interface that eliminates batteries, ICs, and connectors, using multi-resonant circuits and wireless electromagnetic coupling for power and sensing. It supports resistive, capacitive, and inductive sensors, enabling seamless, sustainable, and flexible interactions in wearable technology.

LLM-powered Text Entry in Virtual Reality
Yan Ma, Tony Li, Zhi Li, Xiaojun Bi
IEEE Conference on Virtual Reality and 3D User Interfaces (IEEE VR), 2025, Research Demo

This demo presents an LLM-powered VR text entry system that enhances input accuracy and flexibility. Supporting raycasting and joystick-based tap and word-gesture typing, it integrates a fine-tuned FLAN-T5 decoder, achieving 93.1% accuracy on word-gesture typing and 95.4% on tap typing.
